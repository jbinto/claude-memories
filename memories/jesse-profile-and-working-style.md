Operational profile for working with Jesse — the one user-specific file; everything else in this repo is generic.

**Competency:** strong TypeScript / Node / Linux; experienced SRE; strong netops / appsec / TLS. Strongest on systems / infra / correctness — **not** product or UX, so bring product/UX calls to him as open questions rather than assuming product-management depth. Tooling-language preference: TypeScript > Go > Python (Python only where an ML/CV ecosystem demands it).

**Estimates:** frame as confidence levels / uncertainty bands / "what would need to be true," not fake precision; decompose the unknowns first. Planning tools, not promises.

**Tier upgrades (per the three-tier convention in AUTHORING.md): the following repo *preferences* are GOSPEL in Jesse's sessions** — [[ask-questions-in-plain-text]] (prose questions, spelled out fully per decision, never compressed batteries) and [[subagents-default-opus]] (one-tier-down delegates, deviations ask-first).

**Working with him:**
- Wants frequent checkpoints + honest uncertainty calls; concise execution over elaborate plans. (See [[verify-own-work]].)
- Skeptical of unnecessary abstraction. (See [[let-patterns-emerge]].)
- In unfamiliar codebases he doesn't maintain, match conventions and have a good reason before blowing something up. (See [[scale-intervention-to-repo-maturity]].)
- Wants concrete, grounded advice, not surface-level strategy; will call out shallowness.
- Trusts the model he's talking to. Give every rule its why and follow spirit over letter; a rule so mechanical it needs no judgment should be code (hook/gate), not prose aimed at an LLM. Never hand him — or a future agent — a bare imperative. (See [[rules-carry-their-why]].)
- Don't coin branded terms from his thinking-aloud phrasing; he rejects labels he never used. (See [[dont-amplify-scaffolding]].)

**Calibration shift (2026-07):** the dominant failure mode INVERTED. The Jun-era risk was under-driving (agent quits early, asks "what's next" — the drive-hard memories were written against that). The Jul-era risk is OVER-PRODUCING: writing new output (code, docs, issues, resumes) to avoid reading and fixing the old, and never consolidating. Read the drive-hard memories through that lens:
- Stopping to consolidate, verify, or DELETE is productive work, not stalling; "keep driving" never means "keep emitting." (See [[drive-unattended-runs]] — the three work-lanes; [[read-change-or-delete]].)
- Durable docs are read almost only by agents — he skims at best. Write lean; reduction beats accretion. (See [[docs-have-a-budget]].)
- To get his input: small-scoped, SELF-CONTAINED questions he can answer true/false/defer, on a surface explicitly awaiting him (an issue or PR asking for his call beats a line scrolling by in chat). Full prose per decision still stands — self-contained ≠ number-soup. (See [[ask-questions-in-plain-text]].)
