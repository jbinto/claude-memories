**Name durable work by what it *is*, not by where it sits in a sequence.** In any doc that outlives the session, give a recurring capability, concept, or system a **capability noun** in `SCREAMING-KEBAB-CASE` (`LOUDNORM`, `TOMBSTONE`, `PUBLISH-FLOW` — illustrative). Never a sequential label: `Phase 1/2/3`, `Sprint A/B/C`, `Slice 6.5`, `v0/v1`, `task 3.2`.

**Why — a sequence label feels like compression but is actually *substitution*:** the label stands in for the capability instead of naming it, and it smuggles three lies past every future reader.
1. **No semantic hint.** `Sprint C` could be anything; you have to already know what it means. A capability noun gestures at the topic and — the real payoff — is **greppable, globally unique, and visually distinct** in prose. (Be honest: `LOUDNORM` isn't fully self-documenting either — but it gives you something `sprint8.z` never will.)
2. **Fake organization.** Putting a slot number on a hand-wavy decision makes a doc *look* planned. The label becomes the productive-looking thing while the judgment underneath stays vague — the numbering masks the vagueness.
3. **Lies about shape, and smuggles order.** `Sprint B` and `Sprint G` read as comparable units; one might be four hours and one four months (recolour-a-label vs. train-a-model). And numbering asserts "B follows A" without ever arguing for it — a plan snuck past the reader. Three turns later everyone treats that order as gospel, long after the real dependencies shifted or never existed.

**Default — two kinds of handle, distinguishable on sight:**

- **Durable capability / concept / system** → a bare **capability noun** in `SCREAMING-KEBAB` (`LOUDNORM`, `PUBLISH-FLOW`). Reads as: this recurs, persists, will be referenced — so *name it with rigor*: choose carefully, avoid colliding with a handle already in play. This is Ubiquitous Language in the Domain-Driven-Design sense — a term naming a real, persistent thing still meaningful in 3 weeks or 3 years.
- **Short-to-medium-term side-quest, investigation, or speculative idea** → a **date-prefixed handle** (`JUN12-PEAKS-DRAIN`, `MAY27-DEP-UPG` — a month-day prefix). Reads as: ephemeral, carries a best-before date — so *name it cheaply* and expect it to expire. The date does two jobs: it flags the handle **on sight** as the disposable kind, and a `JUN12-` handle encountered in October is self-evidently stale, so a reader knows to question or retire it.

The distinction is **visual and immediate** — you classify a handle by its *form* before reading a word of its definition, and the two forms carry deliberately different rigor: careful-and-persistent vs. cheap-and-expirable. The prefix is a **type tag, not a timestamp for its own sake**; it also sidesteps the monotonic-counter problem (a counter like `TASK-12` just smuggles the fake linear order back in). The test for which kind to reach for: *does the weight of the name match the weight of the thing?* — the effort you spend naming it should match how long it will live.

**Rigor relaxes as the scope gets more ephemeral.** A counter is poison in a *durable* handle — `TASK-12` smuggles fake linear order into something that persists and gets over-indexed as gospel — but it's harmless once a date prefix fences it in. A single chat's microtask dump can be `JUN12-BUGBASH`, and numbering *within* it is fine: `JUN12-BUGS-004-NESTING`. The `JUN12-` prefix alone is what quarantines it — the date already marks the whole handle as expirable, so nothing under it can leak into durable docs or be read as a project plan. The deeper the ephemeral scope, the cheaper the naming — shortcuts banned at the durable tier become safe once a date stamp fences them off.

**A handle has to resolve to a meaning, or its greppability buys nothing** — you've only traded "`Sprint C` means nothing" for "`LOUDNORM` — go find out." So define every handle somewhere durable and findable, and make a dead one recognizable as dead. **Scale the mechanism:** a handful of handles → gloss each at first use (or a short list in the doc); once they proliferate → a dedicated `glossary.md` (repo root or `docs/`). Capability nouns get a real one-line description; date-prefixed handles can be terser but still get a line with **status** (open / done / abandoned) — they're *built* to go stale, so the status is what lets a future reader retire them.

**Data entities are the exception** — write them lowercase (`mix`, `show`; in code, follow the language convention). They appear too often in prose for SCREAMING-KEBAB to keep its punch, and code naming already disambiguates them. General form: when a name could mean either a cross-cutting *concept* or its concrete *implementation*, **LOUD-TERM means the concept, lowercase means the implementation.**

**Consider — boundaries that keep this from over-firing:**
- **Ordering work is fine, often necessary.** The antipattern isn't sequence — it's *encoding* sequence into durable labels. Plain language between named capabilities is correct: "LOUDNORM goes before PUBLISH-FLOW," "FLOWERPOWER blocks on COFFEEMAKER." Two traps: don't number things that are actually *independent* (an arbitrary order hardens into gospel), and don't conflate *session-local* sequencing (fine inside one working run) with *project-level* dependency tracking (a different problem with different tools — Gantt-chart territory).
- **Live conversation is exempt — anything goes.** "We can't do that until LOUDNORM ships" is fine in chat. The line that matters is chat → durable doc: a handle that came up casually must not be promoted into a README without being given a real name. (See [[dont-amplify-scaffolding]].)
- **Mint sparingly.** Coining a term has a cost; too many and you bury the reader (and yourself) in handles. Don't name what doesn't need a name.
- **Hygiene — scrub before it lands.** Convert or delete any session-local phase/sprint labels before they reach a durable doc. Cleaning them up *after* they've propagated across a doc tree is brutal: one such cleanup burned hundreds of thousands of tokens of search-replace and still missed cases. The upfront discipline is far cheaper than the retroactive hunt.
